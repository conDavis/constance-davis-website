📜 the rules of the game

john conway created this cellular automaton in 1970. every cell looks at its eight neighbors (the cells touching it, including diagonals). then the whole grid updates at once, following three rules:

● survival: a live cell with 2 or 3 live neighbors stays alive ● birth: an empty cell with exactly 3 live neighbors becomes alive ● death: a live cell with fewer than 2 or more than 3 neighbors dies

cyan cells are newborns, pink ones have stuck around a bit, red and grape have been here a while.

❓ why?

one of the first times i really lost myself in a programming project was implementing conway's game of life in my high school computer science class. there was something so incredible about a handful of rules producing organic-looking patterns that could persist or burn out. i still think it's beautiful to watch.
